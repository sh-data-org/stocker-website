## The difference is the earnings input

A trailing P/E uses historical earnings, commonly from the most recent twelve months. A forward P/E uses estimated future earnings. Both divide a share price by earnings per share, but they answer different questions.

The first relates today's price to reported results. The second relates it to a forecast. A lower forward multiple can reflect expectations for higher earnings; it is not proof that those expectations will be achieved.

## Hold the share price constant

Consider a fictional company with a $60 share price, $3 in trailing earnings per share and a $4 estimate for the future period:

| Measure      | Calculation | Result |
| ------------ | ----------- | -----: |
| Trailing P/E | $60 ÷ $3    |     20 |
| Forward P/E  | $60 ÷ $4    |     15 |

Nothing about the share price changed between these calculations. The earnings input changed. If the future estimate later falls to $2, the forward ratio becomes 30 at the same $60 price.

This is why a comparison should show the inputs and periods rather than simply selecting the smaller multiple.

## Ask what the forecast assumes

An estimate can depend on sales growth, margins, financing costs, taxes and share count. You do not need to build a detailed model immediately, but you should know which assumptions drive the direction.

Check the estimate's date and whether it is a consensus or a particular analyst's number. A forecast made before a major announcement may not reflect the latest information.

Forward estimates for different businesses also need aligned periods. “Next year” can refer to different fiscal calendars. Reported and adjusted earnings can differ even when the dates look similar.

## Do not turn a ratio into a verdict

A high multiple may reflect optimistic expectations, while a low multiple can reflect uncertainty or declining earnings. The ratio does not tell you by itself whether either expectation is reasonable.

For a loss-making company, conventional P/E comparison may not be meaningful at all. Read our [negative P/E explanation](/learn/negative-pe-ratio/) before trying to compare negative multiples.

## Calculate, label and investigate

Use the [P/E calculator](/tools/pe-ratio-calculator/) with your own inputs and record whether the earnings are historical or estimated. The calculator does not fetch forecasts or decide which basis is appropriate.

When using Stocker or another research app, check how each field is defined before comparing it with a number elsewhere. An unexplained difference is a question to investigate, not evidence that one provider must be wrong.
