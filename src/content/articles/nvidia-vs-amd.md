## Compare the businesses first

Nvidia and AMD both participate in computing markets, but they are not interchangeable businesses. Shared themes such as AI or gaming can hide differences in products, customers, software and the way each company reports results.

This article gives you a research framework. It does not choose which stock to buy, forecast returns or present a live valuation comparison. Any current investment judgment needs up-to-date prices, filings and assumptions.

## Where their activities overlap

Nvidia describes products and platforms across data center, gaming, professional visualization and automotive. AMD's annual reporting describes Data Center, Client and Gaming, and Embedded activities.

| Research dimension   | Nvidia                                                           | AMD                                                  |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Broad lens           | Accelerated-computing products and platforms                     | A portfolio spanning computing and graphics products |
| Areas to examine     | Data-center platforms, graphics, software and networking context | Data center, client/gaming and embedded product mix  |
| Comparison challenge | Understand how hardware and the surrounding platform connect     | Understand how different end markets contribute      |
| Primary evidence     | Company filings and segment discussion                           | Company filings and segment discussion               |

Reporting labels are not standardized comparison buckets. Do not assume a similarly named market has the same revenue composition or economics in both companies.

## Three questions that improve the comparison

**What does the customer actually buy?** A component, a broader system and supporting software can create different relationships with the customer. Explain the product being compared before treating two revenue figures as equivalent.

**What drives demand?** Identify the spending decision behind the revenue. A consumer replacing a device and a data-center operator expanding capacity are different customers with different planning cycles.

**What must happen next?** Write down the assumptions behind your view, including product adoption, competition, customer spending and supply availability. Then identify evidence that could challenge those assumptions.

## Separate operating progress from valuation

A business can deliver growth while its stock disappoints if the price already reflected stronger expectations. Conversely, a slower-growth business is not automatically unattractive at every price. Business quality and investment price are separate research questions.

Use consistent reporting periods and accounting measures when comparing [revenue and earnings](/learn/revenue-vs-earnings/). If you compare P/E ratios, align the earnings basis and understand why [trailing and forward figures](/learn/trailing-vs-forward-pe/) differ.

## Build a comparison you can revisit

Save the companies you want to study and keep a dated [research worksheet](/tools/research-template/) for each. Record the source, the comparison period and the unanswered question beside every important observation.

Start with [how Nvidia makes money](/companies/how-nvidia-makes-money/), then read AMD's original reporting. A clear explanation of their differences is a more useful first outcome than an unsupported prediction of which stock will win.
