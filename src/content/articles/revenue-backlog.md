Revenue backlog generally describes contracted or booked work that a company has not yet recognized as revenue. It can help you understand future activity, but its value depends on the company’s definition, delivery schedule and customers’ ability to change their orders.

Start with the definition in the company’s own report. One business may count firm purchase orders. Another may include contract options or work awaiting funding. Two backlog figures can look comparable while measuring different things.

## Backlog, revenue and cash answer different questions

| Number                            | Question it helps answer                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Backlog                           | What work is booked for future delivery under this company’s definition?                                        |
| Recognized revenue                | What sales did the company record in this reporting period?                                                     |
| Cash collected                    | How much money has the company actually received?                                                               |
| Remaining performance obligations | What transaction price is allocated to obligations still to be satisfied under the disclosed accounting policy? |

A customer may pay a deposit before delivery, or pay after the company completes the work. Backlog alone does not tell you which arrangement applies. Read [revenue vs. earnings](/learn/revenue-vs-earnings/) alongside the cash-flow statement before using a single metric to judge the business.

## A simple backlog calculation

Consider a fictional equipment manufacturer. It starts a quarter with $100 million of booked work. During the quarter it receives $40 million of new orders, recognizes $30 million of revenue from the order book, and removes $10 million of canceled orders.

**Ending backlog = $100m + $40m − $30m − $10m = $100m.**

The ending figure is unchanged, although the company added orders, delivered equipment and lost business. A flat headline can hide substantial movement.

This is an illustrative reconciliation. Real disclosures may include currency movements, acquisitions, scope changes or adjustments to previously estimated contract values. Some businesses also recognize revenue from work that never appeared in the prior period’s backlog.

## Read the conversion schedule

Ask how much backlog management expects to turn into revenue over the next year, and how much sits further out. A multiyear contract should not be treated as next quarter’s sales.

Write the expected conversion period beside the backlog figure. If the company changes that timetable, investigate the explanation: customer postponements, production limits and a shift toward longer contracts have different implications.

A large order book can support planning, but it can also require materials, staff and working capital before cash arrives. Delivery capacity matters alongside demand.

## Check cancellation rights and profitability

A booked order can be delayed, reduced or canceled. [Company filings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1577445/000149315226037619/ex99-1.htm) explicitly describe these risks. Some government contracts also depend on future funding or optional extensions.

Backlog does not show the margin the company will earn. A fixed-price contract can remain in the order book while rising delivery costs make it less attractive. Look for the company’s discussion of cost estimates and execution risks rather than assuming that a bigger backlog means higher profits.

## Why backlog may differ from remaining performance obligations

Read the reconciliation when a company provides both measures. [CACI’s annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/275880/000095017024025359/2023_ars.pdf), for example, explains differences involving options, task orders and anticipated contract execution or funding. That is evidence about CACI’s definition, not a rule to apply automatically to another company.

## A five-line research note

Before adding a company to your watchlist, record:

1. The backlog amount and measurement date.
2. Exactly what the definition includes and excludes.
3. The share expected to become revenue within the next year.
4. Cancellation, funding and delivery conditions.
5. How the definition and conversion schedule compare with the prior report.
