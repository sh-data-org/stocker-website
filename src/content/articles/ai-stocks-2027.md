Start with three questions for every company on your AI stock watchlist. What does it sell, who pays for it, and where does that money show up in the filings? Those answers make it easier to compare businesses that play very different roles in the same data center.

## Start with three parts of the AI supply chain

| Business | What to look for in the filings |
|---|---|
| **Chip designers** sell processors, networking products or complete systems. | Look for disclosed data center sales, margin changes and dependence on major customers or suppliers. |
| **Manufacturing and component suppliers** make chips, memory or other components. | Look for the customers or end markets they disclose, how much they’re spending to expand capacity and when that capacity is expected to come online. |
| **Cloud providers** sell access to computing power and related services. | Check cloud revenue and capital spending. Read how management explains which services drove sales and what the company is building. |

A company can play more than one role. For each watchlist entry, write one sentence about the product or service you’re researching. That gives you something specific to look for in the next earnings report.

## Check how the company reports its AI exposure

Open the annual report and read the business description, the segment note and management’s discussion of the results. Copy the company’s exact revenue label and reporting period into your notes. If the company provides a broader figure, record what that figure includes. [SEC guide to reading annual reports](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/how-read)

NVIDIA is a useful example. Its fiscal 2026 filing lists **Compute & Networking** as a reportable segment and **Data Center** as a market platform. The filing also names TSMC and Samsung as foundries that make its wafers. You can record Data Center revenue under its reported label and separately note the company's reliance on outside manufacturing. [NVIDIA fiscal 2026 annual filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)

## Compare what would move each business

Imagine a chip supplier reports higher data center sales while a cloud provider increases spending on servers. For the supplier, check whether margins held up as sales grew. For the cloud provider, watch whether cloud revenue and operating cash flow grow as the new capacity comes online. Both may be tied to the same buildout, with different costs and timing.

Give each company one thing to verify at the next report. Put it next to the date and the original filing so you can compare the evidence when the company reports again.

- **Company and ticker** — the business you’re following.
- **Product and customer** — what it sells and who buys it.
- **Evidence** — the reported metric, period and source.
- **Risk** — one dependency or cost that could change your view.
- **Next check** — what you’ll look for in the next report.
