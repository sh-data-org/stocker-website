One AI data center can buy a chiller, a coolant distribution unit and the labor to connect them. That spending shows up differently for each supplier. Vertiv, Modine and Carrier sell cooling equipment. Comfort Systems USA gets paid to build, install and service mechanical systems. Here’s what each sells and how much of the cooling business you can see in its filings.

## Who makes coolant distribution units for data centers?

Vertiv, Modine’s Airedale brand and Carrier all sell **coolant distribution units (CDUs)**. In a common direct-to-chip setup, a cold plate draws heat from a processor into circulating liquid. A liquid-to-liquid CDU pumps that coolant around the server loop and transfers its heat into a separate facility loop. A chiller can cool the facility loop. Some designs use other equipment to get rid of that heat. ## Vertiv vs. Modine in data center cooling

**Vertiv sells cooling and power infrastructure.** Its 2025 annual filing describes air and liquid cooling products, rack power distribution and services. The September 2026 quarterly filing groups sales into the Americas, Asia Pacific, and Europe, Middle East & Africa. Each region sells a mix of equipment and services, with no separate liquid cooling revenue line in that filing. When management reports growth, look for which products or services it identifies as the drivers. [Vertiv 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1674101/000167410126000008/vrt-20251231.htm) · [Vertiv September 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1674101/000162828026067893/vrt-20260930.htm)

**Modine gives readers a dedicated Data Centers segment to track.** It includes chillers, air handlers, CDUs, rear-door heat exchangers and immersion cooling equipment. The segment reported **$439.7 million in net sales** for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, up **95%** from a year earlier. Gross margin fell from **24.4% to 19.7%** as material costs rose and supplier constraints complicated the production ramp. The segment includes both air and liquid cooling products. For the next report, watch whether margins recover as shipments grow. [Modine September 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/67347/000110465926122523/mod-20260930x10q.htm)

## Carrier and Comfort Systems show two other ways to follow cooling demand

**Carrier sells commercial cooling equipment and service.** Its QuantumLeap data center portfolio includes chillers, air handlers, CDUs and aftermarket support. Carrier’s 2025 annual report puts **global data center revenue at $1 billion** for that year. That is management’s figure for the end market. Its formal climate segments also serve commercial and residential customers, so those segment totals cover much more. [Carrier 2025 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783180/000178318026000018/carrier-2025xannualreport.pdf) · [Carrier September 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783180/000178318026000037/carr-20260930.htm) · [Carrier QuantumLeap product overview](https://www.carrier.com/commercial/en/us/news/news-article/new-carrier-coolant-distribution-unit-expands-data-center-solutions.html)

**Comfort Systems USA sells the contracting work.** On large jobs, its mechanical teams can install chillers, air handlers, cooling towers and the piping that connects them. In its September 2026 filing, it pointed to strong data center demand and discussed growth across mechanical, modular and electrical operations. To follow that business, look at how the company describes new project bookings and the timing of work in its backlog. The filings group cooling installation with other contracting services. [Comfort Systems USA 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1035983/000110465926017530/fix-20251231x10k.htm) · [Comfort Systems USA September 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1035983/000110465926118159/fix-20260930x10q.htm)
