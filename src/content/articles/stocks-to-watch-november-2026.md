## The November watchlist

**Nvidia, Target and Walmart are three stocks to watch in November 2026 because their companies have scheduled earnings calls for November 17, 18 and 19.** Each event gives you a specific reason to revisit the business, rather than collect a ticker from a headline.

If you searched for the best stocks to buy in November, start by separating a useful research opportunity from an attractive purchase price. A scheduled report provides new information. It does not establish that a stock is cheap, suitable for your portfolio or about to rise.

| Company | Ticker | Scheduled earnings call     | Research focus                             |
| ------- | ------ | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Nvidia  | NVDA   | November 17, 5 p.m. Eastern | Demand, margins and customer spending      |
| Target  | TGT    | November 18, 8 a.m. Eastern | Sales quality, inventory and profitability |
| Walmart | WMT    | November 19, 8 a.m. Eastern | Sales growth, mix and operating profit     |

Dates and call times were checked against the company sources below on November 9. Recheck the original event page before relying on a schedule. A call time is different from the time results are released.

## Nvidia: test the demand story

[Nvidia's announcement](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Sets-Conference-Call-for-Third-Quarter-Financial-Results/default.aspx) schedules its fiscal 2027 third-quarter call for November 17 at 2 p.m. Pacific, or 5 p.m. Eastern. The quarter ended October 25, 2026; the fiscal-year label does not mean the report covers calendar 2027.

Before the release, write down what you want to learn about demand and profitability. Afterward, separate the reported quarter from management's outlook for the next one. Look for changes in the explanation, not only a higher revenue figure.

**A useful question:** are sales, margins and the outlook telling a consistent story?

**What could weaken the case:** slower customer spending, a difficult product transition or a less profitable sales mix. These are questions to investigate, not claims that those developments have occurred.

## Target: look beyond a sales headline

[Target's investor calendar](https://corporate.target.com/investors/events-presentations) lists its third-quarter 2026 call for November 18, from 8 to 9 a.m. Eastern.

Use the report to investigate how sales translate into profit. If the release discusses comparable sales, customer traffic or transaction size, keep their definitions beside the figures. Growth from more visits can mean something different from growth from larger purchases.

**A useful question:** do inventory, promotions and margins support the sales story?

**What could weaken the case:** sales that require heavier discounting, inventory that becomes harder to sell or costs growing faster than revenue. Read management's explanation and the financial statements together before drawing a conclusion.

## Walmart: compare growth with its economics

[Walmart schedules its fiscal 2027 third-quarter call](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/events/fy2027-q3-earnings-release) for November 19 at 7 a.m. Central, or 8 a.m. Eastern. Its event page says earnings materials are expected at approximately 6 a.m. Central.

The research job is to connect the growth headline with operating profit and cash generation. Note which segments and activities explain a change, and distinguish reported figures from currency-adjusted or adjusted measures.

**A useful question:** does stronger activity also improve profitability, or does it require more spending?

**What could weaken the case:** an unfavorable sales mix, additional operating costs or a valuation that already assumes better results. A familiar business can still be an expensive stock.

## A simple before-and-after worksheet

Before each event, save one question, the prior reporting period and the source you plan to read. After the release, record the actual result and whether it answered your question.

| Before the report              | After the report                            |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| What is my research question?  | Which reported fact answers it?             |
| What expectation am I testing? | Was that expectation supported or weakened? |
| Which risk matters most?       | Did the explanation of that risk change?    |
| What information is missing?   | What should I check next?                   |
