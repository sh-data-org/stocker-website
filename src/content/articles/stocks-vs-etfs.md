## A company and a fund are different objects

An individual stock represents an ownership interest in a company. An exchange-traded fund, or ETF, pools investors' money and holds assets according to its strategy. Those assets might include stocks, bonds or other exposures.

Both can trade on an exchange, but buying one fund share is not the same research decision as selecting one operating company. You need to understand what the fund owns and how it is managed.

## Compare the questions, not just the price

| Research area        | Individual stock                                | ETF                                                  |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| What am I examining? | One company's business and finances             | A fund's holdings and investment approach            |
| What drives results? | The company's economics and market expectations | The portfolio, strategy, costs and market conditions |
| What should I read?  | Company filings and investor materials          | Prospectus, holdings and fund disclosures            |
| What can be hidden?  | Business-specific risks                         | Concentration, overlap and strategy complexity       |

A lower share price does not establish that either product is cheaper in an economic sense. The units and underlying exposures differ.

## Diversification depends on the holdings

A broad fund can spread exposure across many businesses. But the letters ETF do not guarantee broad diversification. Some funds focus on a narrow industry, a small number of holdings or a specialized strategy.

Check actual positions and weights. Two funds with different names can still have substantial overlap. A fund you already hold may also own a company you are researching individually, increasing your exposure to the same business if you add the stock.

Leveraged and inverse products introduce additional complexity. They should not be treated as interchangeable with a conventional broad-market fund simply because all are exchange traded.

## Costs and trading details matter

Read the fund's expense information and consider trading costs and the bid-ask spread. ETF market prices can differ from the value of the underlying holdings. Past performance does not establish what future returns will be.

For an individual company, the research focus shifts toward business performance, financial strength, valuation assumptions and company-specific risks. Neither approach eliminates the possibility of loss.
