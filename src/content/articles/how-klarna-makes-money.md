**The retailer can pay Klarna a fee even when the shopper pays zero interest.** With Pay in 4, Klarna settles with the retailer and the shopper repays Klarna in four installments. The merchant fee pays for the transaction and for offering the customer a way to spread out the purchase. [Klarna’s business description](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2003292/000200329225000052/klarnagroupplc424b4.htm)

## Follow a $100 Pay in 4 purchase

Suppose a shopper spends $100 at a participating store and makes every payment on time. Assume the retailer agreed to a $4 fee for this example. Actual merchant fees depend on the agreement.

| Who          | What happens                                                                                                                       |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Shopper**  | Pays four installments of $25, for $100 total and $0 in interest.                                                                  |
| **Retailer** | Receives $96 after the example merchant fee. It can settle the sale before the shopper finishes paying.                            |
| **Klarna**   | Recovers the $100 purchase balance as installments come in. The $4 spread is the fee before funding, processing and credit losses. |

If the store gains a sale it would otherwise have missed, it can weigh that extra sale against the fee. For Klarna, the balance still has to be funded between settlement and the shopper’s final payment. Its annual report describes consumer deposits as a major source of funding and shows funding costs and credit losses separately from revenue. [Klarna’s FY2025 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2003292/000162828026038366/annualreport2025.htm)

## Where the merchant fee shows up in Klarna’s accounts

Klarna recorded **$3.51 billion** in group revenue for 2025. The filing shows how payments, consumer services and financing contribute to that total.

| Revenue category                     |         FY2025 | What it covers                                                    |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------: | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Transaction revenue                  | $2,103 million | Merchant revenue and advertising revenue across Klarna’s network. |
| Consumer service revenue             |   $397 million | Certain fees paid by shoppers, including reminder fees.           |
| Gain on sale of consumer receivables |    $73 million | Gains on sales of Fair Financing receivables.                     |
| Interest income                      |   $937 million | Income that includes interest from longer-term financing.         |

Figures are rounded independently. The merchant fee from the example belongs in transaction revenue. That line also includes other products and fees, so it can’t tell you how much Klarna made specifically from Pay in 4 or what it charged a particular merchant. [Klarna’s FY2025 annual report, Note 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2003292/000162828026038366/annualreport2025.htm)

## How Pay in 4 differs from Fair Financing

Pay in 4 gives the shopper a short repayment schedule with zero interest. **Fair Financing** typically spreads repayments over a longer period and can charge interest depending on the offer. That changes how long Klarna funds the balance and how it earns money. When reading Klarna’s next report, check the mix of short installments and longer financing alongside funding and credit costs. Revenue growth tells you only part of the story.
