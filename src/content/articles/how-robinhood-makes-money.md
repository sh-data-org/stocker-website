## Robinhood revenue breakdown

Robinhood (Nasdaq: HOOD) makes money from trading activity, net interest, and subscriptions and services. A US customer can place an eligible stock trade without paying a commission, while Robinhood can still earn money from the market maker that receives the order. Trading-related revenue was about **61%** of its total net revenues in the quarter ended September 30, 2026.

| Reported revenue           |            Q3 2026 | Share of total |
| -------------------------- | -----------------: | -------------: |
| Transaction-based revenues |       $867 million |            61% |
| Net interest revenues      |       $415 million |            29% |
| Other revenues             |       $142 million |            10% |
| **Total net revenues**     | **$1.424 billion** |       **100%** |

Robinhood reported $62 million in Gold subscription revenue within Other revenues for the quarter. [Robinhood’s Q3 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783879/000178387926000167/hood-20260930.htm)

## How Robinhood makes money on trades

**Payment for order flow**, or PFOF, is compensation a market maker pays a broker for routing orders to it. Robinhood describes fees from routing equity and options orders as PFOF. The broker receives the payment from the market maker, while the customer receives an execution price for the order. How the broker routes the order matters because it can affect the price a customer gets.

The transaction category also includes activity such as crypto and event contracts. When trading revenue grows, check which products drove that change. In Q3 2026, Robinhood reported higher options, equities, and event-contract revenue alongside lower crypto revenue than a year earlier. [Robinhood’s quarterly results](https://investors.robinhood.com/news-releases/news-release-details/robinhood-reports-third-quarter-2026-results)

## How interest and Robinhood Gold contribute

**Net interest revenues** reflect interest earned after related interest expenses, plus the net contribution from securities lending. Robinhood earns interest from sources such as margin loans and cash and deposits. The result can change with interest rates, how much customers borrow, and how much cash and other interest-earning assets the business holds.

**Robinhood Gold** brings in subscription fees. The company reports those fees separately within Other revenues, which also includes sources such as proxy services. Gold subscription revenue rose from $47 million in Q3 2025 to $62 million in Q3 2026. Compare that figure with subscriber growth to see how the subscription business is developing. [Robinhood’s Q3 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783879/000178387926000167/hood-20260930.htm)

## What to track for HOOD stock research

1. **Trading mix.** Read the revenue breakdown for equities, options, crypto, and event contracts. A strong quarter in one product can mask weaker activity elsewhere.
2. **Interest and customer balances.** Check what drove the change in net interest. Management may point to rates, growth in interest-earning assets, or securities lending.
3. **Subscriptions and risk.** Track Gold revenue and subscribers, and read the filing’s discussion of order routing and regulatory exposure. The [guide to reading 10-K risk factors](/learn/how-to-read-10-k-risk-factors/) shows how to turn those disclosures into research questions.
