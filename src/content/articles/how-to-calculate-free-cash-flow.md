## Start with a simple definition

One common free cash flow calculation takes operating cash flow and subtracts capital expenditure. The SEC notes that definitions differ. Write down exactly which line you use for each input.

## Work through Costco’s FY2026 cash flow

The filing reports $15.817 billion in operating cash flow and $6.435 billion in additions to property and equipment. On this definition, the calculation gives $9.382 billion. Treat that as a calculation from the filing, rather than a company-reported metric.

## Watch the sign and the period

The statement often shows capital spending in parentheses because it is a cash outflow. Subtract the positive amount of the outflow. Use the same fiscal period and units in both inputs.

## Keep the calculation with your notes

The result still leaves other cash obligations to consider. If you compare companies, check how each handles leases or other adjustments before comparing reported figures. Record the formula and the original filing in the [research worksheet](/tools/research-template/).
