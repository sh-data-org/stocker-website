## Start with the level of detail

A sector is a broad grouping of economic activity. An industry is a narrower grouping of businesses with more specific activities in common. Labels help organize research, but they do not replace an explanation of what a company actually does.

The Global Industry Classification Standard, or GICS, uses four levels: sector, industry group, industry and sub-industry. Other data providers can use different taxonomies or naming conventions. A classification label in an app is not automatically a GICS label.

## An organizing example

Think of classification as a map with different zoom levels. At the widest view, you find a broad part of the economy. Zoom in and you find a narrower activity, then more specific businesses.

| Level of your question | What you are trying to understand                                        |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Broad sector           | Which part of economic activity interests me?                            |
| Industry               | Which businesses provide a similar kind of product or service?           |
| Individual company     | Who pays this business, and what are its particular strengths and risks? |

Two businesses can belong to the same sector and still have different customers, capital requirements and profit margins. The label is an organizing starting point, not evidence that their financial ratios should be identical.

## Use peers carefully

When comparing two companies, ask whether their products, customers and economics really overlap. A broad category may put very different activities together. Conversely, a company can compete with businesses whose primary classifications differ.

A sensible peer comparison explains the reason for including each company. “Both appear in a technology list” is a weaker justification than a description of the product markets where they actually compete.

Our [Nvidia vs AMD comparison](/compare/nvidia-vs-amd/) illustrates how shared markets can coexist with different product mixes. It is a business comparison, not a ranking of expected stock returns.

## A sector choice is not a diversification plan

Selecting several interests can broaden the companies you discover. It does not establish that your investments are diversified. Different companies may depend on the same customer spending, supply chain or economic conditions, even when labels differ.

Keep a distinction between using categories to find ideas and allocating actual money. The latter also depends on the rest of a portfolio, objectives and risk constraints.
