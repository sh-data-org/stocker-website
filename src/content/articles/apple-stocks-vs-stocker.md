## The short answer

Apple Stocks is a convenient starting point when you already know which symbols you want to follow on your iPhone. Stocker focuses on discovering companies through a swipe-based experience, then saving the ones that interest you for further research.

Neither is a brokerage. The choice is about how you want to find and organize information, not where you execute a trade. You may find the built-in app sufficient, or use different tools for different parts of your routine.

## Discovery and research

If your question is “What happened to the companies I already follow?”, a familiar list can be the most direct route. Apple documents symbol search, charts, details and news within its Stocks app.

Stocker starts from a different moment: “Which company should I understand next?” Its discovery experience introduces companies, while its research screens give you more context before you decide whether to save one.

Discovery is not a recommendation to buy. A personalized sequence can reflect what interests you without establishing whether a security fits your finances or is attractively valued.

## Watchlists and expectations

A saved list is a research aid. It does not automatically become a portfolio tracker with cost basis, brokerage reconciliation or performance reporting. Be clear about which job you need before choosing a product.

Stocker's public listing describes free discovery and a saved watchlist, with Premium features available. Review current limits, prices and trial eligibility in the app before subscribing. This page does not use an old price or a promotional offer as a permanent comparison point.

## Which fits your routine?

Choose a starting point by the task, not by a blanket winner. If a built-in symbol list and quick information checks solve your problem, begin there. If you want to explore unfamiliar companies and turn that curiosity into a research list, try Stocker's discovery flow.

For deeper company work, both should lead you back to primary filings. Our [research checklist](/learn/how-to-research-stocks/) explains what to inspect after a company catches your attention.
